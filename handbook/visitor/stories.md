# Stories

Stories are the media side of WHOISWHO: interviews, features, spotlights, opinion, announcements and news about the people and ideas on the record. A story is an article. The Stories page is laid out like a magazine, not a blog archive.

## Read stories

1. Open Stories from the header.
2. Choose a type tab (All, Interview, Feature, Spotlight, Opinion, Announcement, News) and, if you like, an Industry.
3. Browse the large lead tile, the grid below it and the rest of the stories, newest first.
4. Open a story. Each card shows the type, date, reading time, headline, a one-line lede and the author. Reading time is worked out from the word count.

Events are not on the Stories page. They have their own pages.

## A single story

| Block | Shown when |
|---|---|
| Type, date, industry, headline, lede, author, cover image, body | Always |
| Sponsored badge and notice | The story has a live sponsored placement |
| Profiles in this story ("On the record") | The story links to public profiles. A profile that is not public is not shown. |
| Related event | The story links a published event |
| Comments | Always |
| More stories | Always, three other stories |

A story that is not published returns "not found" to visitors.

## Sign up for new stories

1. Enter an email address in the sign-up form on the Stories page or the Home page.
2. Tick the consent text.
3. Confirm the address from the email you receive.
4. Every marketing email carries an unsubscribe link. One click unsubscribes, with no account.

Staff side: [Newsletter](/staff/newsletter).

## Comment on a story

1. Enter a name, an email address and tick the consent box. There is no account.
2. Post the comment. The visitor sees "Thank you. Your comment will appear once it is approved."
3. An Editor or Administrator approves or removes it. Only approved comments appear.

The email address is never shown. Gravatar is off, and comments are not offered on profiles.

## Sponsored stories

A sponsor can pay for a story to sit in a labelled slot on Home, Stories or both, for set dates. The card and the story page show "Sponsored" with the sponsor's name and this notice: "Sponsored content. Paid for by (sponsor). The sponsor did not write it and has no influence over any label or award." The slot never reorders other stories or search results. When the dates end, the label and slot go. See [Trust and independence](/concepts/trust-and-independence).
